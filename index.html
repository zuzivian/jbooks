    Sources and method

    • Sources. The Comptroller's R-1 and P-1 exhibit spreadsheets for each budget request, listed in sources.json. FY2026 materials are indexed at comptroller.war.gov/Budget-Materials/Budget2026. Each row's "Source" link is the exact file it was read from, and data/lines.json records a SHA-256 hash of that file.
    • Screen. A row is kept if its program-element or budget-line title matches a keyword. The core list is UAS, unmanned aircraft, counter-UAS, C-UAS, counter-small UAS, drone, loitering munition and Replicator. The extended list adds UAV, unmanned aerial, RPA, uncrewed, collaborative combat aircraft, MQ-/RQ- designations, a few platform names, and abbreviated forms such as "CTR SM UNMAN AIR". Extended hits catch more lines and more noise. Matching ignores case. A line is tagged C-UAS if a counter keyword matches; otherwise it's tagged UAS.
    • Review. overrides.json holds hand-reviewed decisions, each with a reason. It can drop a false hit or add a line no keyword caught. Dropped lines stay listed in the data file so nothing disappears silently.
    • Amounts. Figures are copied as printed, in thousands of dollars. Each file's parsed column sums are reconciled against the exhibit's own "Total of Displayed Rows" before anything is kept. A P-1 budget line can span several rows, one per cost type (for example advance procurement). Its "Non-Add" memo rows are shown but not summed. A cell that isn't a plain number is left out of the row and listed in unreadable. Nothing is estimated or filled in.
    • Known misses. UAS money buried inside broader lines (aircraft spares, air-defence sensors, classified aggregates) is invisible at line level, as are platform names not on the watch list. The exhibits are line-level, so a mixed line counts at its full value.
    • Not a tally. Totals shown here are the sum of the matched lines. They are not DoD's count of UAS or C-UAS spending.
